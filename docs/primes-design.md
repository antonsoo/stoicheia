# IX.20: design before implementation

The reader is fluent in English and beginning Greek/Latin. The main task is to
discover why a non-prime sum still completes Euclid's prime argument. The
opening six-prime list produces 30031; a trial bound of 13 deliberately leaves
it unresolved. Extending to 100 reveals 59 × 509. These are exact numerical
examples, not Euclid's printed givens.

The most characteristic object is the last complete group of p units, followed
by one differently marked unit. The reader chooses a given prime to change the
size of that group. Exact integer equations sit beside it. No decorative number
cloud, prime-grid wallpaper, fake ancient texture or animated factoring feed.

```text
One more. Must it be prime?       Opening question / short direction
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Given primes [2 3 5 7 11 13]     Editable list / three purposeful cases
M = product                     Exact construction
N = M + 1                       Exact construction
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Division ledger                 Last p units / one added unit
choose p                        Text equivalent / exact copies
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Try divisors through [13]        Unresolved / prime / composite
extend to [100]                  Branch argument and usable witness
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Any finite list                 Reason independent of numerical bound
VII.31 descent                  Choose any proper divisor of 2310
Parallel Greek / Latin / English + contextual word + actual pages
Source viewer                   Whole pages / inspect, pan and retry
Retained reading                Native JSON/CSV/SVG and complete evidence
```

All text is left-aligned, with a maximum reading measure. The workbench is a
division ledger rather than equal-sized cards. Mobile stacks construction,
ledger and unit microscope in reading order. Source controls use native selects
and buttons. Color always has text/shape reinforcement. Readings use sentence
case labels, rather than repeating the earlier rooms' all-capital eyebrows.

The generic initial idea of a prime generator was rejected: it would reward
large numbers rather than explain the composite branch. The search explicitly
retains unresolved cofactors. A discovered prime can join the given list, but
the proof does not require the app to factor every sum. The separate divisor
descent exposes the existence lemma behind that distinction.

The scan's p.389 line orders Δ–Ε–Ζ, while the text names ΔΖ as the unit and ΕΖ
as the sum. We show that discrepancy in the reading note and retain the entire
page. Modern M/N labels avoid presenting the printed line as a faithful scale
diagram. When p exceeds 29, the app gives exact text instead of drawing a false
one-to-one unit picture.
