# A construction the reader has to make

The course's I.2 plate reveals a prepared construction. The desk asks the
reader to choose centres, points, lines and crossings instead. Its one task
is to place at A a length equal to the detached BC, using the restricted
circle operation already explained in the course. There is no radius field,
length-transfer tool or freely placed new point.

The visual focus is a large working compass drawing. Keep the course's blue
ink (#193d60), pale blue ground (#e8eff4), white drawing surface (#fbfcfe),
violet choice marks (#694780) and muted slate (#52677b). Give the target
remainder a separate deep green (#17634d). Use the bundled Noto Serif for
Greek, source passages and geometric labels; use the existing system face
for controls. Avoid another collection of identical lesson cards.

Desktop: drawing at left, a narrow instrument tray at right. The transcript
runs below the drawing, rather than covering the geometry. On phones put
the short instrument tray first, with a link between it and the drawing.
All actions have native form controls; tapping SVG points is a shortcut.

Task and allowed operation

The initial plan included a generic geometry toolbar. Replace it with
three explicit operations and one task. The constraint is the lesson:
circles must be centred at an existing point and pass through another.
Joining and continuing a straight line combine postulates 1 and 2; this
modern interface choice must be stated. “Collapsing compass” describes an
operation restriction, not an assertion about an ancient physical compass.

Do not award a proof from near-equal measured lengths. Recognize the I.2
incidence chain and the two beyond-endpoint choices, show its equal wholes
and equal parts, and say when another construction has no recognized chain.
The intersection engine uses floating-point real-plane coordinates; it is
not a formal axiomatic prover. Saved readings carry the actual transcript,
all intermediate diagrams, source text and checked page images.
