# Circle workshop design

Subject: the directed rational approximations in Archimedes' _Measurement of
a Circle_ 3. Audience: an English-speaking learner with right-triangle geometry
and no prior Greek. The page's job is to make the difference between a plausible
number and a justified bound operable.

The existing Stoicheia identity takes precedence over a new theme. Tokens:
paper `#e8eff4`, ink `#193d60`, subdued ink `#52677b`, plate `#fbfcfe`,
outer polygon `#694780`, inner polygon `#146d72`. Noto Serif carries the
source languages and restrained display text; system sans carries instructions;
tabular system monospace carries exact arithmetic. No new font download.

The signature is a pair of perimeter bounds with a magnified interval. Both
polygons retain equal scales. At 96 sides, their visual coincidence cannot serve
as evidence: the rational ledger remains legible. A failed square comparison
breaks the proof chain visibly, even if later local comparisons pass.

    circle and two polygons | choose bisection / proof direction
    inner bound < C/d < outer bound, with exact fractions
    exact arithmetic ledger | alter one printed approximation
    geometric reason -> Greek / Latin / English -> complete source pages
    save the experiment, its rejected steps, reading and evidence

The first design idea used two large numeric tiles. Rejected: they made the
outcome look like two independently correct calculator answers. The shared
interval and dependency ledger carry the argument instead. No ornamental
animation; bisection responds directly to a control. On a phone, controls precede
the drawing and the audit remains a native horizontally scrollable table.
