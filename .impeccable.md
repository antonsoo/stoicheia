## Design Context

### Users

English-speaking learners exploring Euclid while beginning ancient Greek and
Latin. The owner's brief asks for source-grounded geometry, useful illustrations,
and discoveries that reward investigation. The owner has authorized independent
design decisions while unavailable.

### Brand Personality

Scholarly, tangible, inquisitive. Make the reader feel able to check an argument,
including the places where a convincing picture hides an assumption.

### Aesthetic Direction

A working geometric plate beside a readable bilingual edition, using the
existing pale blue paper, dark ink and locally bundled serif. Prefer large,
carefully labeled constructions and typographic hierarchy to decorative cards.
Keep the source visible and distinguish a modern experiment from its ancient text.

### Design Principles

- A control should answer a geometric question, not merely change a picture.
- Keep impossible and limiting cases inspectable; never repair givens silently.
- Make Greek, Latin and English readable at phone width, with keyboard access.
- Draw lengths at equal scales; use text as well as color to explain relations.
- Save useful readings that continue to work without a connection.
