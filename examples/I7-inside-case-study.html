The printed part relation no longer fits

At Γ, ray ΓΒ lies outside ∠ΑΓΔ. So ∠ΔΓΒ is not a part of ∠ΑΓΔ. Their numerical sizes can still have the desired order; that does not repair this reason for the order. We need a different containing angle.

∠ΑΓΔ = ∠ΑΓΒ − ∠ΔΓΒ

Use the angles below the base

Extend ΑΓ beyond Γ to Ε, and ΑΔ beyond Δ to Ζ (postulate 2). In isosceles ΑΓΔ, I.5 makes the two exterior angles ΕΓΔ and ΖΔΓ equal. Ε and Ζ are labels for this supplement; they do not occur in the printed I.7 passage.

∠ΕΓΔ = ∠ΖΔΓ (I.5, exterior angles)

Carry the order through equal angles

At Γ, ∠ΔΓΒ is now a proper part of ∠ΕΓΔ. At Δ, ∠ΖΔΓ is a proper part of ∠ΓΔΒ. The equal exterior angles sit between the smaller and larger angles. This uses whole and part, substitution of equal magnitudes, and transitivity of order.

∠ΔΓΒ < ∠ΕΓΔ = ∠ΖΔΓ < ∠ΓΔΒ

The second equality would contradict this order

If ΒΓ also equalled ΒΔ, I.5 in triangle ΒΓΔ would make ∠ΔΓΒ equal to ∠ΓΔΒ. The previous comparison made them unequal. That is the contradiction. The sketch retains the first equality and displays the actual unequal second pair; it cannot draw both incompatible assumptions as facts.

If ΒΓ = ΒΔ: ∠ΔΓΒ = ∠ΓΔΒ. But ∠ΔΓΒ < ∠ΓΔΒ.

Read the clause that makes the repair possible

The base of the isosceles triangle ΑΓΔ is ΓΔ. “Below the base” means the angles made by extending the equal sides past Γ and Δ; it does not mean lower on the screen.

Τῶν ἰσοσκελῶν τριγώνων αἱ πρὸς τῇ βάσει γωνίαι ἴσαι ἀλλήλαις εἰσίν, καὶ προσεκβληθεισῶν τῶν ἴσων εὐθειῶν αἱ ὑπὸ τὴν βάσιν γωνίαι ἴσαι ἀλλήλαις ἔσονται.

In triangulis aequicruriis anguli ad basim positi inter se aequales sunt, et productis rectis aequalibus anguli sub basi positi inter se aequales erunt.

In isosceles triangles the angles at the base are equal to one another; when the equal sides are extended, the angles below the base will also be equal to one another.

ὑπὸ τὴν βάσιν / sub basi: “below the base.” In the angle name ἡ ὑπὸ ΑΓΔ, the letters instead identify the two bounding rays, with Γ as vertex.
