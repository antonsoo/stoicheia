Use the segment, not a flattened triangle

Δ lies between Β and Γ. Thus ΒΓ consists of ΒΔ together with the nonzero ΔΓ, and is greater than ΒΔ. The second equality is impossible directly by whole and part. Applying I.5 to ΒΓΔ here would use a degenerate triangle.

ΒΓ = ΒΔ + ΔΓ > ΒΔ

Read the clause that makes the repair possible

The base of the isosceles triangle ΑΓΔ is ΓΔ. “Below the base” means the angles made by extending the equal sides past Γ and Δ; it does not mean lower on the screen.

Τῶν ἰσοσκελῶν τριγώνων αἱ πρὸς τῇ βάσει γωνίαι ἴσαι ἀλλήλαις εἰσίν, καὶ προσεκβληθεισῶν τῶν ἴσων εὐθειῶν αἱ ὑπὸ τὴν βάσιν γωνίαι ἴσαι ἀλλήλαις ἔσονται.

In triangulis aequicruriis anguli ad basim positi inter se aequales sunt, et productis rectis aequalibus anguli sub basi positi inter se aequales erunt.

In isosceles triangles the angles at the base are equal to one another; when the equal sides are extended, the angles below the base will also be equal to one another.

ὑπὸ τὴν βάσιν / sub basi: “below the base.” In the angle name ἡ ὑπὸ ΑΓΔ, the letters instead identify the two bounding rays, with Γ as vertex.
