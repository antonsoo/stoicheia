STOICHEIA · EUCLID, BOOK I

I.2 · Carry a length without carrying the compass

To place, at a given point, a straight line equal to a given straight line.

ΒΓ = ΒΗ · ΔΛ = ΔΗ; ΔΑ = ΔΒ · ΔΛ − ΔΑ = ΔΗ − ΔΒ · ΑΛ = ΒΗ = ΒΓ

The proposition

Πρὸς τῷ δοθέντι σημείῳ τῇ δοθείσῃ εὐθείᾳ ἴσην εὐθεῖαν θέσθαι.

Ad datum punctum datae rectae aequalem rectam constituere.

To place, at a given point, a straight line equal to a given straight line.

1. A point and a separate length Given

Ἔστω τὸ μὲν δοθὲν σημεῖον τὸ Α, ἡ δὲ δοθεῖσα εὐθεῖα ἡ ΒΓ· δεῖ δὴ πρὸς τῷ Α σημείῳ τῇ δοθείσῃ εὐθείᾳ τῇ ΒΓ ἴσην εὐθεῖαν θέσθαι.

Sit datum punctum Α, data autem recta ΒΓ. oportet igitur ad punctum Α datae rectae ΒΓ aequalem rectam constituere.

Let Α be the given point and ΒΓ the given straight line. We must place at Α a straight line equal to ΒΓ.

μέν … δέ pairs two items: the given point on the one hand, the given line on the other. δοθέν and δοθεῖσα both mean ‘given’; their forms agree with the different nouns.

2. Join Α to Β Construct

Ἐπεζεύχθω γὰρ ἀπὸ τοῦ Α σημείου ἐπὶ τὸ Β σημεῖον εὐθεῖα ἡ ΑΒ,

ducatur enim a puncto Α ad Β punctum recta ΑΒ,

Draw the straight line ΑΒ from point Α to point Β.

ἐπεζεύχθω is singular: one line is to be joined. Compare the plural ἐπεζεύχθωσαν in I.1, where two lines were joined.

3. Use I.1 on the new line Construct

καὶ συνεστάτω ἐπ᾽ αὐτῆς τρίγωνον ἰσόπλευρον τὸ ΔΑΒ.

et in ea construatur triangulus aequilaterus ΔΑΒ,

On it construct the equilateral triangle ΔΑΒ.

ἐπ᾽ αὐτῆς and in ea both mean ‘on it’, referring back to ΑΒ. The point name Δ is delta; this triangle supplies the equality ΔΑ = ΔΒ.

4. Extend past Α and Β Construct

καὶ ἐκβεβλήσθωσαν ἐπ᾽ εὐθείας ταῖς ΔΑ, ΔΒ εὐθεῖαι αἱ ΑΕ, ΒΖ,

et producantur in directum rectae ΔΑ, ΔΒ, ut fiant ΑΕ, ΒΖ,

Continue ΔΑ and ΔΒ in straight lines beyond Α and Β, forming ΑΕ and ΒΖ.

ἐκβεβλήσθωσαν commands that the lines be extended. Latin producantur in directum says ‘let them be extended straight on’. The directions matter: go away from Δ.

5. Mark the reference length from Β Construct

καὶ κέντρῳ μὲν τῷ Β διαστήματι δὲ τῷ ΒΓ κύκλος γεγράφθω ὁ ΓΗΘ.

et centro Β radio autem ΒΓ circulus describatur ΓΗΘ,

With centre Β and radius ΒΓ, draw circle ΓΗΘ. Η lies on the extension beyond Β.

Η is eta, not the Latin letter H. The circle is named by points on its circumference; Θ is another such point in the source figure. This plate labels the points needed for the argument.

6. Reach both extended sides from Δ Construct

καὶ πάλιν κέντρῳ τῷ Δ καὶ διαστήματι τῷ ΔΗ κύκλος γεγράφθω ὁ ΗΚΛ.

et rursus centro Δ radio autem ΔΗ circulus describatur ΗΚΛ.

Again, with centre Δ and radius ΔΗ, draw circle ΗΚΛ. Λ lies on the extension beyond Α.

Λ is lambda. The names ΗΚΛ identify the second circle. Κ is not needed in the subtraction argument, so the plate shows the working arc through Η and Λ.

7. Identify the first equal radii Prove

Ἐπεὶ οὖν τὸ Β σημεῖον κέντρον ἐστὶ τοῦ ΓΗΘ, ἴση ἐστὶν ἡ ΒΓ τῇ ΒΗ.

iam quoniam Β punctum centrum est circuli ΓΗΘ, erit ΒΓ = ΒΗ.

Since Β is the centre of circle ΓΗΘ, ΒΓ equals ΒΗ.

The radius argument from I.1 appears again. ΒΓ is the given length, and ΒΗ is now an equal length on the extended side.

8. Identify equal wholes and equal parts Prove

πάλιν, ἐπεὶ τὸ Δ σημεῖον κέντρον ἐστὶ τοῦ ΗΚΛ κύκλου, ἴση ἐστὶν ἡ ΔΛ τῇ ΔΗ, ὧν ἡ ΔΑ τῇ ΔΒ ἴση ἐστίν.

rursus quoniam Δ punctum centrum est circuli ΗΚΛ, erit ΔΛ = ΔΗ, quarum partes ΔΑ, ΔΒ aequales.

Since Δ is the centre of circle ΗΚΛ, ΔΛ equals ΔΗ; within them, the parts ΔΑ and ΔΒ are equal.

ὧν means ‘of which’. Heiberg’s Latin makes the implied relationship explicit with quarum partes: ‘whose parts’. The equal parts come from the equilateral triangle.

9. Subtract the equal parts Prove

λοιπὴ ἄρα ἡ ΑΛ λοιπῇ τῇ ΒΗ ἐστιν ἴση.

itaque ΑΛ = ΒΗ.

Therefore the remainder ΑΛ is equal to the remainder ΒΗ.

λοιπή means ‘remaining’. Greek names the remainders, while the Latin prints an equation. The language encodes an operation: remove ΔΑ and ΔΒ, leaving ΑΛ and ΒΗ.

10. Connect the remainder to the reference Prove

ἐδείχθη δὲ καὶ ἡ ΒΓ τῇ ΒΗ ἴση· ἑκατέρα ἄρα τῶν ΑΛ, ΒΓ τῇ ΒΗ ἐστιν ἴση. τὰ δὲ τῷ αὐτῷ ἴσα καὶ ἀλλήλοις ἐστὶν ἴσα· καὶ ἡ ΑΛ ἄρα τῇ ΒΓ ἐστιν ἴση.

sed demonstratum est ΒΓ = ΒΗ. itaque utraque ΑΛ, ΒΓ rectae ΒΗ aequalis est. uerum quae eidem aequalia sunt, etiam inter se aequalia sunt. ergo etiam ΑΛ = ΒΓ.

ΒΓ was also shown equal to ΒΗ. Since ΑΛ and ΒΓ are each equal to ΒΗ, they are equal to one another: ΑΛ = ΒΓ.

ἑκατέρα means ‘each of the two’; Latin utraque has the same role. The proof repeats common notion 1 rather than relying on the appearance of the diagram.

11. The new length starts at Α Conclude

Πρὸς ἄρα τῷ δοθέντι σημείῳ τῷ Α τῇ δοθείσῃ εὐθείᾳ τῇ ΒΓ ἴση εὐθεῖα κεῖται ἡ ΑΛ· ὅπερ ἔδει ποιῆσαι.

Ergo ad datum punctum Α datae rectae ΒΓ aequalis constituta est recta ΑΛ; quod oportebat fieri.

At the given point Α, the straight line ΑΛ has been placed equal to the given straight line ΒΓ: which was to be done.

κεῖται (‘lies’ or ‘has been placed’) answers the opening task θέσθαι (‘to place’). Latin constituere is answered by constituta est. The end echoes the beginning.

The length survives because equal parts disappear

The equilateral triangle looks like a detour. Its purpose is to create equal removable parts. ΔΛ and ΔΗ are radii of one circle; removing equal ΔΑ and ΔΒ leaves equal ΑΛ and ΒΗ. ‘Collapsing compass’ is a modern description of the restricted operation, not evidence about an actual ancient instrument. Change ΒΓ or flip the triangle: the subtraction still works.
