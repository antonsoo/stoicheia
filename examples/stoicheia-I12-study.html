STOICHEIA · EUCLID, BOOK I

I.12 · Drop a perpendicular from outside the line

Draw a perpendicular straight line to a given unbounded straight line from a given point not on it.

Why the other side matters

The circle passes through Δ beyond the line, while Γ is its centre on this side. Its radius therefore exceeds the centre's distance to ΑΒ, ensuring two distinct crossings. Move Δ: the chord changes, while the perpendicular foot stays fixed.

ΕΘ = ΘΗ (I.10) · ΗΘ = ΘΕ; ΘΓ is common · ΓΕ = ΓΗ (radii); ∠ΕΘΓ = ∠ΗΘΓ (I.8) · ΓΘ is perpendicular (definition 10)

The proposition

Ἐπὶ τὴν δοθεῖσαν εὐθεῖαν ἄπειρον ἀπὸ τοῦ δοθέντος σημείου, ὃ μή ἐστιν ἐπ᾽ αὐτῆς, κάθετον εὐθεῖαν γραμμὴν ἀγαγεῖν.

Ad datam rectam infinitam a dato puncto extra eam sito perpendicularem rectam lineam ducere.

Draw a perpendicular straight line to a given unbounded straight line from a given point not on it.

1. Take the line and an exterior point Given

Ἔστω ἡ μὲν δοθεῖσα εὐθεῖα ἄπειρος ἡ ΑΒ τὸ δὲ δοθὲν σημεῖον, ὃ μή ἐστιν ἐπ᾽ αὐτῆς, τὸ Γ· δεῖ δὴ ἐπὶ τὴν δοθεῖσαν εὐθεῖαν ἄπειρον τὴν ΑΒ ἀπὸ τοῦ δοθέντος σημείου τοῦ Γ, ὃ μή ἐστιν ἐπ᾽ αὐτῆς, κάθετον εὐθεῖαν γραμμὴν ἀγαγεῖν.

Sit data recta infinita ΑΒ punctum autem datum extra eam situm Γ. oportet igitur ad datam rectam infinitam ΑΒ a dato puncto extra eam sito Γ perpendicularem rectam ducere.

Let ΑΒ be the given unbounded line and Γ the given point outside it. We must draw a perpendicular from Γ to ΑΒ.

ἄπειρον / infinitam means the line is not limited to the displayed endpoints. ὃ μή ἐστιν ἐπ᾽ αὐτῆς is a relative clause: ‘which is not on it’. Latin compresses that condition to extra eam situm.

Reason: The task and its given objects A given object is part of the problem. It has not been constructed by this proof. A finite straight line here means a line segment.

Printed source: Greek pp. 34; Latin pp. 35.

2. Choose Δ on the other side Construct

Εἰλήφθω γὰρ ἐπὶ τὰ ἕτερα μέρη τῆς ΑΒ εὐθείας τυχὸν σημεῖον τὸ Δ,

sumatur enim in altera parte rectae ΑΒ quoduis punctum Δ,

Choose a point Δ on the other side of the straight line ΑΒ.

τυχὸν / quoduis means ‘any’, but the following placement restriction still applies. ἐπὶ τὰ ἕτερα μέρη / in altera parte puts Δ across the line from Γ. It does not mean anywhere in the plane.

Reason: Choose the auxiliary point on the other side Δ must lie across ΑΒ from Γ. Then the distance ΓΔ is greater than the perpendicular distance from Γ to the line, so the circle has two distinct crossings in the Euclidean plane. Euclid uses that crossing without separately proving a continuity axiom. A same-side choice loses the guarantee: the circle can miss, touch, or still cross the line.

Printed source: Greek pp. 34; Latin pp. 35.

3. Draw the circle through Δ Construct

καὶ κέντρῳ μὲν τῷ Γ διαστήματι δὲ τῷ ΓΔ κύκλος γεγράφθω ὁ ΕΖΗ,

et centro Γ radio autem ΓΔ circulus describatur ΕΖΗ,

With center Γ and distance ΓΔ, draw the circle ΕΖΗ. Its crossings with ΑΒ are Ε and Η.

κέντρῳ μὲν … διαστήματι δέ distinguishes center from distance. ΕΖΗ names the circle through points on it; Ζ is not another center. The English makes the two crossings explicit, as the source's next command and diagram require them.

Reason: Postulate 3 · draw a circle A circle may be described with a centre and a distance. Here the distance is from the centre to an already available point; drawing this circle does not silently carry a length to a different centre.

Printed source: Greek pp. 34; Latin pp. 35.

4. Construct the chord's midpoint Construct

καὶ τετμήσθω ἡ ΕΗ εὐθεῖα δίχα κατὰ τὸ Θ,

et recta ΕΗ in duas partes aequales secetur in Θ,

Bisect the straight line ΕΗ at Θ.

τετμήσθω is a command to have the bisection completed. κατὰ τὸ Θ / in Θ names where it is made. I.10 supplies that construction; the foot is not selected by measuring a right angle.

Reason: I.10 · bisect the chord ΕΗ Ε and Η are the two intersections of the circle with the unbounded line. Apply I.10 to their segment: its equilateral and bisector constructions locate Θ. The model calls that construction rather than assigning the perpendicular foot by projection.

Printed source: Greek pp. 34; Latin pp. 35.

5. Join the radii and the proposed perpendicular Construct

καὶ ἐπεζεύχθωσαν αἱ ΓΗ, ΓΘ, ΓΕ εὐθεῖαι· λέγω, ὅτι ἐπὶ τὴν δοθεῖσαν εὐθεῖαν ἄπειρον τὴν ΑΒ ἀπὸ τοῦ δοθέντος σημείου τοῦ Γ, ὃ μή ἐστιν ἐπ᾽ αὐτῆς, κάθετος ἧκται ἡ ΓΘ.

et ducantur rectae ΓΗ, ΓΘ, ΓΕ. dico, ad datam rectam infinitam ΑΒ a dato puncto Γ extra eam sito perpendicularem ductam esse ΓΘ.

Join ΓΗ, ΓΘ and ΓΕ. I claim that ΓΘ is perpendicular to the given line ΑΒ from its exterior point Γ.

The plural command ἐπεζεύχθωσαν / ducantur joins three pairs of points. Two resulting lines are radii; ΓΘ is the line whose perpendicularity still needs proof.

Reason: Postulate 1 · join two points A straight line may be drawn from any point to any point. The straightedge is unmarked: this operation does not measure a length.

Printed source: Greek pp. 34; Latin pp. 35.

6. Use the constructed halves and common side Prove

Ἐπεὶ γὰρ ἴση ἐστὶν ἡ ΗΘ τῇ ΘΕ, κοινὴ δὲ ἡ ΘΓ, δύο δὴ αἱ ΗΘ, ΘΓ δύο ταῖς ΕΘ, ΘΓ ἴσαι εἰσὶν ἑκατέρα ἑκατέρᾳ·

nam cum ΗΘ = ΘΕ, et communis sit ΘΓ, duae rectae ΗΘ, ΘΓ duabus ΕΘ, ΘΓ aequales sunt altera alteri.

ΗΘ equals ΘΕ, and ΘΓ is common. Thus ΗΘ and ΘΓ equal ΕΘ and ΘΓ respectively.

Heiberg prints δύο ταῖς here, with δυσί recorded in the apparatus for other witnesses; the main-text wording is retained. Latin duabus makes the comparison with the second pair explicit. The equality of the halves comes from I.10.

Reason: The constructed halves and their common side I.10 supplied ΗΘ = ΘΕ. ΘΓ belongs to both triangles. No angle equality has been used to place Θ.

Printed source: Greek pp. 34; Latin pp. 35.

7. Complete the three-side comparison Prove

καὶ βάσις ἡ ΓΗ βάσει τῇ ΓΕ ἐστιν ἴση· γωνία ἄρα ἡ ὑπὸ ΓΘΗ γωνίᾳ τῇ ὑπὸ ΕΘΓ ἐστιν ἴση. καί εἰσιν ἐφεξῆς.

et basis ΓΗ basi ΓΕ aequalis est. itaque ∠ ΓΘΗ = ΕΘΓ. et deinceps positi sunt.

The base ΓΗ equals ΓΕ. Therefore angle ΓΘΗ equals angle ΕΘΓ, and these angles are adjacent.

The middle letter Θ identifies the common vertex of both angles. ΓΗ and ΓΕ are equal because they are radii, not because Θ was already assumed to be the perpendicular foot.

Reason: I.8 · equal radii complete the side comparison ΓΗ and ΓΕ are radii of the same circle. Together with ΗΘ = ΘΕ and common ΘΓ, I.8 gives equal angles ΓΘΗ and ΕΘΓ. Their other rays run in opposite directions on the given straight line.

Printed source: Greek pp. 34; Latin pp. 35.

8. Name the right angles and perpendicular Prove

ὅταν δὲ εὐθεῖα ἐπ᾽ εὐθεῖαν σταθεῖσα τὰς ἐφεξῆς γωνίας ἴσας ἀλλήλαις ποιῇ, ὀρθὴ ἑκατέρα τῶν ἴσων γωνιῶν ἐστιν, καὶ ἡ ἐφεστηκυῖα εὐθεῖα κάθετος καλεῖται ἐφ᾽ ἣν ἐφέστηκεν.

ubi autem recta super rectam lineam erecta angulos deinceps positos inter se aequales efficit, rectus est uterque angulus aequalis, et recta linea erecta perpendicularis adpellatur ad eam, super quam erecta est.

When a line standing on a line makes adjacent angles equal, each is right, and the standing line is called perpendicular to the line on which it stands.

καλεῖται / adpellatur means ‘is called’: the definition names a relationship now established. ἐφεστηκυῖα is the perfect participle ‘having stood upon’; Latin erecta plays the corresponding descriptive role.

Reason: Definition 10 · equal adjacent angles are right A line standing on a line makes equal adjacent angles. Definition 10 calls each right and the standing line perpendicular. The construction earns the angle equality first; the measured 90° readout is a check, not the reason.

Printed source: Greek pp. 34; Latin pp. 35.

9. The perpendicular has been obtained Conclude

Ἐπὶ τὴν δοθεῖσαν ἄρα εὐθεῖαν ἄπειρον τὴν ΑΒ ἀπὸ τοῦ δοθέντος σημείου τοῦ Γ, ὃ μή ἐστιν ἐπ᾽ αὐτῆς, κάθετος ἧκται ἡ ΓΘ· ὅπερ ἔδει ποιῆσαι.

Ergo ad datam rectam infinitam ΑΒ a dato puncto Γ extra eam sito perpendicularis ducta est ΓΘ. quod oportebat fieri.

Thus ΓΘ has been drawn perpendicular to the given unbounded line ΑΒ from the given point Γ outside it. The requested construction is complete.

The conclusion repeats the exterior-point condition. Changing the auxiliary Δ changes the circle and chord; with the source's opposite-side choice, their constructed midpoint still yields the same perpendicular from Γ.

Reason: The construction is complete The requested object has been constructed and its defining equality has been proved. The closing phrase says that the task was to make something, rather than merely state a theorem.

Printed source: Greek pp. 34; Latin pp. 35.

‘Any point’ still has a condition

Move Δ while keeping it on the other side of ΑΒ from Γ. The radius and two intersections change, but the foot stays fixed. Deliberately move Δ to the same side: a circle can miss the line, touch it once, or still cross twice. Only two distinct intersections supply a nonzero chord for I.10. The source's placement condition guarantees that case.

Definitions and starting permissions used in this proof

Postulate 3 · γʹ / III · Draw a circle

Καὶ παντὶ κέντρῳ καὶ διαστήματι κύκλον γράφεσθαι.

Et ut quouis centro radioque circulus describatur.

And that a circle can be drawn with any centre and distance.

κέντρῳ and διαστήματι are datives; Latin centro and radio are ablatives. The two languages attach the circle-drawing operation to a centre and a distance. Compare the fuller command Κέντρῳ … διαστήματι … γεγράφθω in I.1.

Printed source: Greek pp. 8; Latin pp. 9.

Postulate 1 · αʹ / I · Join two points

Ἠιτήσθω ἀπὸ παντὸς σημείου ἐπὶ πᾶν σημεῖον εὐθεῖαν γραμμὴν ἀγαγεῖν.

Postuletur, ut a quouis puncto ad quoduis punctum recta linea ducatur.

Let it be postulated that a straight line can be drawn from any point to any point.

Ἠιτήσθω / Postuletur means ‘let it be requested / postulated’. The Greek then uses an infinitive, ἀγαγεῖν (‘to draw’); Latin uses ut … ducatur, ‘that … be drawn’. The following postulates continue the opening request.

Printed source: Greek pp. 8; Latin pp. 9.

Definition 15 · ιεʹ / XV · A circle and its equal radii

Κύκλος ἐστὶ σχῆμα ἐπίπεδον ὑπὸ μιᾶς γραμμῆς περιεχόμενον [ἣ καλεῖται περιφέρεια], πρὸς ἣν ἀφ’ ἑνὸς σημείου τῶν ἐντὸς τοῦ σχήματος κειμένων πᾶσαι αἱ προσπίπτουσαι εὐθεῖαι [πρὸς τὴν τοῦ κύκλου περιφέρειαν] ἴσαι ἀλλήλαις εἰσίν.

Circulus est figura plana una linea comprehensa, ad quam quae ab uno puncto intra figuram posito educuntur rectae omnes aequales sunt.

A circle is a plane figure enclosed by one line [called the circumference], to which all the straight lines falling from one point within the figure [to the circumference of the circle] are equal to one another.

The bracketed clauses are printed in Heiberg’s Greek and omitted from his Latin. They are retained here. πᾶσαι … εὐθεῖαι means ‘all the straight lines’; the statement supplies equal radii, not just two radii that happen to look alike.

Printed source: Greek pp. 4; Latin pp. 5.

Definition 10 · ιʹ / X · Right angles and a perpendicular

Ὅταν δὲ εὐθεῖα ἐπ᾽ εὐθεῖαν σταθεῖσα τὰς ἐφεξῆς γωνίας ἴσας ἀλλήλαις ποιῇ, ὀρθὴ ἑκατέρα τῶν ἴσων γωνιῶν ἐστι, καὶ ἡ ἐφεστηκυῖα εὐθεῖα κάθετος καλεῖται, ἐφ᾽ ἣν ἐφέστηκεν.

Ubi uero recta super rectam lineam erecta angulos deinceps positos inter se aequales efficit, rectus est uterque angulus aequalis, et recta linea erecta perpendicularis adpellatur ad eam, super quam erecta est.

When a straight line standing on a straight line makes the adjacent angles equal to one another, each equal angle is right, and the standing line is called perpendicular to the line on which it stands.

The definition crosses a page in both languages. ἐφεξῆς / deinceps positos specifies adjacency, and ἴσας ἀλλήλαις / inter se aequales supplies equality. No degree measure is used in this definition.

Printed source: Greek pp. 2, 4; Latin pp. 3, 5.
