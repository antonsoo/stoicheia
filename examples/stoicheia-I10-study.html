I.10 · Earn a segment’s midpoint

Divide a given finite straight line into two equal parts.

Reuse I.9 with the two circles already drawn

ΓΑ = ΓΒ is already known from I.1. Use Α and Β as I.9’s equal-distance points. The second circle crossing, labelled Ζ₉ only for this explanation, gives its equilateral vertex. The line through Γ and Ζ₉ meets ΑΒ at Δ. Euclid’s I.10 labels only Α, Β, Γ, Δ.

The proposition

Τὴν δοθεῖσαν εὐθεῖαν πεπερασμένην δίχα τεμεῖν.

Datam rectam terminatam in duas partes aequales diuidere.

Divide a given finite straight line into two equal parts.

1. Take the finite segment to be divided Given

Ἔστω ἡ δοθεῖσα εὐθεῖα πεπερασμένη ἡ ΑΒ· δεῖ δὴ τὴν ΑΒ εὐθεῖαν πεπερασμένην δίχα τεμεῖν.

Sit data recta terminata ΑΒ. oportet igitur rectam terminatam ΑΒ in duas partes aequales diuidere.

Let ΑΒ be the given finite straight line. It is to be divided into two equal parts.

πεπερασμένη / terminata identifies a bounded segment. A midpoint belongs to that finite length; it is not the middle of an infinite supporting line. Compare postulate 2, which starts from a finite line and extends it.

Reason: The task and its given objects A given object is part of the problem. It has not been constructed by this proof. A finite straight line here means a line segment.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

2. Construct the equilateral triangle on it Construct

Συνεστάτω ἐπ’ αὐτῆς τρίγωνον ἰσόπλευρον τὸ ΑΒΓ,

construatur in ea triangulus aequilaterus ΑΒΓ,

Construct the equilateral triangle ΑΒΓ on ΑΒ.

αὐτῆς / ea refers back to the given segment. The new construction does not introduce another arbitrary base. I.1 has already established how to make this triangle.

Reason: I.1 · equal sides above the given base Construct the equilateral triangle ΑΒΓ. In particular, ΑΓ = ΒΓ. Its two original circles also provide the other intersection, which can be reused when applying I.9.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

3. Bisect the angle at Γ Construct

καὶ τετμήσθω ἡ ὑπὸ ΑΓΒ γωνία δίχα τῇ ΓΔ εὐθείᾳ·

et angulus ΑΓΒ recta ΓΔ in duas partes aequales diuidatur.

Bisect angle ΑΓΒ with the straight line ΓΔ.

τετμήσθω is the requested division: ‘let it have been cut’. The dative τῇ ΓΔ εὐθείᾳ names the dividing line; Latin recta ΓΔ is ablative. Δ lies where that line meets the given base in the figure.

Reason: I.9 · bisect the angle at Γ Apply the angle-bisector construction at Γ. Here Α and Β are already available points at equal distances from Γ. The other intersection of I.1’s circles completes the equilateral triangle needed by I.9. Join Γ to that intersection; Δ is where the resulting line crosses ΑΒ.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

4. State the proposed midpoint Prove

λέγω, ὅτι ἡ ΑΒ εὐθεῖα δίχα τέτμηται κατὰ τὸ Δ σημεῖον.

dico, rectam ΑΒ in puncto Δ in duas partes aequales diuisam esse.

I claim that Δ divides ΑΒ into two equal parts.

κατὰ τὸ Δ σημεῖον / in puncto Δ locates the division at a point. The source distinguishes this claim from the construction of ΓΔ: the equality of ΑΔ and ΒΔ remains to be proved.

Reason: The task and its given objects A given object is part of the problem. It has not been constructed by this proof. A finite straight line here means a line segment.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

5. Identify the equal sides around Γ Prove

Ἐπεὶ γὰρ ἴση ἐστὶν ἡ ΑΓ τῇ ΓΒ, κοινὴ δὲ ἡ ΓΔ, δύο δὴ αἱ ΑΓ, ΓΔ δύο ταῖς ΒΓ, ΓΔ ἴσαι εἰσὶν ἑκατέρα ἑκατέρᾳ·

nam cum ΑΓ = ΓΒ, et ΓΔ communis sit, duae rectae ΑΓ, ΓΔ duabus ΒΓ, ΓΔ aequales sunt altera alteri;

ΑΓ equals ΓΒ, and ΓΔ is common. Thus ΑΓ and ΓΔ equal ΒΓ and ΓΔ respectively.

ἑκατέρα ἑκατέρᾳ / altera alteri again means ‘each to each’, in the stated order. One pair comes from the equilateral triangle; the second is the same segment ΓΔ used twice.

Reason: Equal equilateral sides and the common bisector I.1 supplies ΑΓ = ΒΓ, while ΓΔ is a common side of ΑΓΔ and ΒΓΔ. The two side pairs have been identified without assuming anything about ΑΔ and ΒΔ.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

6. Use the angle equality just constructed Prove

καὶ γωνία ἡ ὑπὸ ΑΓΔ γωνίᾳ τῇ ὑπὸ ΒΓΔ ἴση ἐστίν·

et ∠ ΑΓΔ = ΒΓΔ.

And the angles ΑΓΔ and ΒΓΔ are equal.

Γ is the middle letter of both angle names, identifying the common vertex. These are the two parts of the angle bisected by I.9, not the angles at the proposed midpoint Δ.

Reason: The angle equality is the result of I.9 I.9 gives ∠ΑΓΔ = ∠ΒΓΔ at Γ. It is the included angle between the two equal side pairs, exactly the angle needed to apply I.4.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

7. Conclude that the two base parts are equal Prove

βάσις ἄρα ἡ ΑΔ βάσει τῇ ΒΔ ἴση ἐστίν.

quare ΑΔ = ΒΔ.

Therefore base ΑΔ equals base ΒΔ.

ἄρα / quare draws the conclusion from I.4’s two sides and included angle. ΑΔ and ΒΔ are called bases of the smaller compared triangles; they together make the original given segment.

Reason: I.4 · equal bases of the two smaller triangles ΑΓ = ΒΓ, ΓΔ is common, and ∠ΑΓΔ = ∠ΒΓΔ. I.4 therefore gives the equal bases ΑΔ and ΒΔ. The midpoint is a consequence of these constructions and equalities, not a coordinate assigned in advance.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

8. The finite line has been bisected Conclude

Ἡ ἄρα δοθεῖσα εὐθεῖα πεπερασμένη ἡ ΑΒ δίχα τέτμηται κατὰ τὸ Δ· ὅπερ ἔδει ποιῆσαι.

Ergo data recta terminata ΑΒ in puncto Δ in duas partes aequales diuisa est; quod oportebat fieri.

The given finite line ΑΒ has therefore been divided into equal halves at Δ. The requested construction is complete.

δίχα τέτμηται / in duas partes aequales diuisa est repeats the task as a completed result. The same language concluded the division of an angle in I.9; the object divided is now a segment.

Reason: The construction is complete The requested object has been constructed and its defining equality has been proved. The closing phrase says that the task was to make something, rather than merely state a theorem.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

Two circles do more work than they first seem to

The two circles used for I.1 have a second intersection. When I.9 is applied at Γ, the already equal segments ΓΑ and ΓΒ let Α and Β serve as its two chosen points. The second crossing completes the needed equilateral triangle without drawing new circles. Joining the two crossings gives the line whose intersection with ΑΒ is Δ. The auxiliary label Ζ₉ identifies the point’s role in I.9; it is not a label added to the source text of I.10.

Definitions and starting permissions used in this proof

Definition 20 · κʹ / XX · Triangles classified by their sides

Τῶν δὲ τριπλεύρων σχημάτων ἰσόπλευρον μὲν τρίγωνόν ἐστι τὸ τὰς τρεῖς ἴσας ἔχον πλευράς, ἰσοσκελὲς δὲ τὸ τὰς δύο μόνας ἴσας ἔχον πλευράς, σκαληνὸν δὲ τὸ τὰς τρεῖς ἀνίσους ἔχον πλευράς.

Ex figuris autem trilateris aequilaterus triangulus est, qui tria latera sua aequalia habet, aequicrurius uero, qui duo sola aequalia habet, scalenus autem, qui tria latera sua inaequalia habet.

Of three-sided figures, an equilateral triangle has its three sides equal; an isosceles triangle has only two sides equal; a scalene triangle has its three sides unequal.

μόνας / sola means ‘only’. This edition’s definition makes the isosceles and equilateral classes separate. Many modern courses include equilateral triangles among isosceles triangles. The conclusion of I.5 remains true when all three sides are equal.
