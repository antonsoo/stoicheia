I.14 · Recover a straight line from its angle condition

If two lines at a point on a line, lying on different sides of it, make adjacent angles equal to two right angles together, then those two lines lie in one straight line.

Hypothetical continuation · not all premises can hold

Ε is drawn off ΓΒΔ, following the printed arrangement. The proof temporarily calls ΓΒΕ one straight line. That claim cannot hold together with the original angle premise and a different Ε. Measurements describe the drawing; conditional equalities describe the rejected supposition.

Given: ∠ΑΒΓ + ∠ΑΒΔ = two right angles · If ΓΒΕ were straight: ∠ΑΒΓ + ∠ΑΒΕ = two right angles · Under that supposition: ∠ΑΒΕ would equal ∠ΑΒΔ · But ∠ΑΒΕ is a proper part of ∠ΑΒΔ → contradiction

The proposition

Ἐὰν πρός τινι εὐθείᾳ καὶ τῷ πρὸς αὐτῇ σημείῳ δύο εὐθεῖαι μὴ ἐπὶ τὰ αὐτὰ μέρη κείμεναι τὰς ἐφεξῆς γωνίας δυσὶν ὀρθαῖς ἴσας ποιῶσιν, ἐπ᾽ εὐθείας ἔσονται ἀλλήλαις αἱ εὐθεῖαι.

Si duae rectae ad rectam aliquam et punctum eius non in eadem parte positae angulos deinceps positos duobus rectis aequales effecerint, in eadem erunt linea recta.

If two lines at a point on a line, lying on different sides of it, make adjacent angles equal to two right angles together, then those two lines lie in one straight line.

1. Keep the two-right-angle premise Given

Πρὸς γάρ τινι εὐθείᾳ τῇ ΑΒ καὶ τῷ πρὸς αὐτῇ σημείῳ τῷ Β δύο εὐθεῖαι αἱ ΒΓ, ΒΔ μὴ ἐπὶ τὰ αὐτὰ μέρη κείμεναι τὰς ἐφεξῆς γωνίας τὰς ὑπὸ ΑΒΓ, ΑΒΔ δύο ὀρθαῖς ἴσας ποιείτωσαν· λέγω, ὅτι ἐπ᾽ εὐθείας ἐστὶ τῇ ΓΒ ἡ ΒΔ.

Nam ad rectam aliquam ΑΒ et punctum eius Β duae rectae ΒΓ, ΒΔ non in eadem parte positae angulos deinceps positos ΑΒΓ, ΑΒΔ duobus rectis aequales efficiant. dico, ΓΒ et ΒΔ in eadem recta esse.

Let ΒΓ and ΒΔ stand on different sides of ΑΒ at Β, making adjacent angles ΑΒΓ and ΑΒΔ equal to two right angles together. I claim that ΓΒ and ΒΔ lie in one straight line.

μὴ ἐπὶ τὰ αὐτὰ μέρη / non in eadem parte excludes placing both outer rays on the same side of ΑΒ. Heiberg's Greek body text prints δύο ὀρθαῖς, while the proposition statement uses δυσίν; the printed forms are retained.

Reason: The task and its given objects A given object is part of the problem. It has not been constructed by this proof. A finite straight line here means a line segment.

Printed source: Greek pp. 38; Latin pp. 39.

2. Suppose a different straight continuation ΒΕ Prove

Εἰ γὰρ μή ἐστι τῇ ΒΓ ἐπ᾽ εὐθείας ἡ ΒΔ, ἔστω τῇ ΓΒ ἐπ᾽ εὐθείας ἡ ΒΕ.

nam si ΒΓ et ΒΔ non sunt in eadem recta, ΓΒ et ΒΕ in eadem recta sint.

If ΒΔ is not in a straight line with ΒΓ, let ΒΕ be the straight continuation of ΓΒ instead.

εἰ … μή / si … non opens a temporary negative supposition. ἔστω / sint introduces its alternative. The hypothetical sketch cannot realize both the given angle condition and a different straight continuation; the plate states that limit explicitly.

Reason: Suppose a different continuation exists The proof supposes ΒΔ is not the continuation of ΓΒ, and names a different continuation ΒΕ. That supposition cannot be drawn together with every original premise. The printed-style sketch keeps the given angle pair and draws Ε separately; it does not actually make Γ, Β and Ε collinear. Its numerical table shows that distinction.

Printed source: Greek pp. 38; Latin pp. 39.

3. Apply I.13 under that temporary claim Prove

Ἐπεὶ οὖν εὐθεῖα ἡ ΑΒ ἐπ᾽ εὐθεῖαν τὴν ΓΒΕ ἐφέστηκεν, αἱ ἄρα ὑπὸ ΑΒΓ, ΑΒΕ γωνίαι δύο ὀρθαῖς ἴσαι εἰσίν·

iam quoniam recta ΑΒ super rectam ΓΒΕ erecta est, ∠ ΑΒΓ + ΑΒΕ duobus rectis aequales sunt.

Under the supposition that ΑΒ stands on the straight line ΓΒΕ, angles ΑΒΓ and ΑΒΕ together equal two right angles.

ἐφέστηκεν / erecta est describes the standing-on relationship assumed in this branch. The English marks its conditional status; the source need not repeat ‘if’ at each consequence of the supposition.

Reason: I.13 · the consequence if ΓΒΕ were one line Under the temporary claim that Γ, Β and Ε are collinear, I.13 would make angles ΑΒΓ and ΑΒΕ equal to two right angles together. This is a conditional assertion in the proof, not a measured property of the hypothetical sketch, whose Ε is deliberately drawn off the line.

Printed source: Greek pp. 38; Latin pp. 39.

4. Compare with the originally given angle pair Prove

εἰσὶ δὲ καὶ αἱ ὑπὸ ΑΒΓ, ΑΒΔ δύο ὀρθαῖς ἴσαι· αἱ ἄρα ὑπὸ ΓΒΑ, ΑΒΕ ταῖς ὑπὸ ΓΒΑ, ΑΒΔ ἴσαι εἰσίν.

uerum etiam ΑΒΓ + ΑΒΔ duobus rectis aequales sunt. itaque ΓΒΑ + ΑΒΕ = ΓΒΑ + ΑΒΔ.

The given pair ΑΒΓ and ΑΒΔ also equals two right angles. Thus ΓΒΑ and ΑΒΕ together would equal ΓΒΑ and ΑΒΔ together.

καί / etiam means ‘also’: it links the conditional total to the original premise. The source changes ΑΒΓ to ΓΒΑ; the outer-letter reversal leaves the same angle.

Reason: The original premise and common notion 1 The original hypothesis gives ΑΒΓ + ΑΒΔ equal to two right angles. The temporary continuation would give ΑΒΓ + ΑΒΕ the same total. Common notion 1 therefore makes those pairs equal under the supposition.

Printed source: Greek pp. 38; Latin pp. 39.

5. Remove the common angle Prove

κοινὴ ἀφῃρήσθω ἡ ὑπὸ ΓΒΑ· λοιπὴ ἄρα ἡ ὑπὸ ΑΒΕ λοιπῇ τῇ ὑπὸ ΑΒΔ ἐστιν ἴση,

subtrahatur, qui communis est, ∠ ΓΒΑ. itaque ∠ ΑΒΕ = ΑΒΔ,

Subtract the common angle ΓΒΑ. The remainder ΑΒΕ would therefore equal the remainder ΑΒΔ.

ἀφῃρήσθω / subtrahatur commands subtraction. λοιπή names what remains after removing the same angle from both sums. The resulting equality is still under the supposition, not a measured equality of the sketch.

Reason: Common notion 3 · subtract the shared angle Remove ΓΒΑ from both conditionally equal pairs. The remainders would be ΑΒΕ and ΑΒΔ. The diagram's measured remainders remain unequal; the proof is showing why the temporary claim cannot coexist with the given premise.

Printed source: Greek pp. 38; Latin pp. 39.

6. The smaller cannot equal the larger Prove

ἡ ἐλάσσων τῇ μείζονι· ὅπερ ἐστὶν ἀδύνατον.

minor maiori; quod fieri non potest.

That would make the smaller equal to the larger, which is impossible.

ἡ ἐλάσσων / minor identifies the smaller angle as the subject; τῇ μείζονι / maiori names the larger one it would equal. ἀδύνατον is negative: impossible. The actual sketch shows the proper-part inequality, not the conditional equality.

Reason: Common notion 5 · a smaller angle cannot equal the larger In the printed arrangement ΑΒΕ is a proper part of ΑΒΔ. The supposed continuation forces them equal, contradicting the whole being greater than its part. The same reasoning excludes any different continuation; ΒΔ is the straight continuation of ΓΒ.

Printed source: Greek pp. 38; Latin pp. 39.

7. Exclude other continuations and retain ΒΔ Prove

οὐκ ἄρα ἐπ᾽ εὐθείας ἐστὶν ἡ ΒΕ τῇ ΓΒ. ὁμοίως δὴ δείξομεν, ὅτι οὐδὲ ἄλλη τις πλὴν τῆς ΒΔ· ἐπ᾽ εὐθείας ἄρα ἐστὶν ἡ ΓΒ τῇ ΒΔ.

quare ΒΕ et ΓΒ non sunt in eadem recta. similiter idem de quauis alia recta praeter ΒΔ demonstrabimus. itaque ΓΒ et ΒΔ in eadem recta sunt.

Therefore ΒΕ cannot be the straight continuation of ΓΒ. Likewise any other line except ΒΔ is excluded. Hence ΓΒ and ΒΔ lie in one straight line.

πλὴν / praeter means ‘except’. The rejection is not merely of the one drawn Ε: ὁμοίως / similiter extends it to any different proposed continuation.

Reason: Common notion 5 · a smaller angle cannot equal the larger In the printed arrangement ΑΒΕ is a proper part of ΑΒΔ. The supposed continuation forces them equal, contradicting the whole being greater than its part. The same reasoning excludes any different continuation; ΒΔ is the straight continuation of ΓΒ.

Printed source: Greek pp. 38; Latin pp. 39.

8. The angle condition forces a straight line Conclude

Ἐὰν ἄρα πρός τινι εὐθείᾳ καὶ τῷ πρὸς αὐτῇ σημείῳ δύο εὐθεῖαι μὴ ἐπὶ τὰ αὐτὰ μέρη κείμεναι τὰς ἐφεξῆς γωνίας δυσὶν ὀρθαῖς ἴσας ποιῶσιν, ἐπ᾽ εὐθείας ἔσονται ἀλλήλαις αἱ εὐθεῖαι· ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι.

Ergo si duae rectae ad rectam aliquam et punctum eius non in eadem parte positae angulos deinceps positos duobus rectis aequales effecerint, in eadem erunt linea recta; quod erat demonstrandum.

Therefore two lines on different sides of a line at its point, making adjacent angles equal to two right angles together, lie in one straight line. This is what had to be shown.

The conclusion restores the full placement condition. I.13 went from a straight line to an angle total; I.14 has proved the converse rather than simply reading the earlier implication backward.

Reason: The construction is complete The requested object has been constructed and its defining equality has been proved. The closing phrase says that the task was to make something, rather than merely state a theorem.

Printed source: Greek pp. 40; Latin pp. 41.

A contradiction sketch cannot satisfy everything

The source supposes a different continuation while retaining the original angle premise. No plane figure can realize that combination. The plate follows the printed arrangement, labels its temporary sketch, and keeps measured angles distinct from the equality that the supposition would force. The original given figure returns when that supposition is rejected.

Definitions and starting permissions used in this proof

Common notion 1 · αʹ / I · Equality through a shared equal

Τὰ τῷ αὐτῷ ἴσα καὶ ἀλλήλοις ἐστὶν ἴσα.

Quae eidem aequalia sunt, etiam inter se aequalia sunt.

Things equal to the same thing are also equal to one another.

τῷ αὐτῷ and eidem mean ‘to the same [thing]’. The Greek neuter plural τὰ … ἴσα takes the singular ἐστίν, a normal Greek construction. Latin uses plural sunt.

Printed source: Greek pp. 10; Latin pp. 11.

Postulate 4 · δʹ / IV · Right angles are equal

Καὶ πάσας τὰς ὀρθὰς γωνίας ἴσας ἀλλήλαις εἶναι.

Et omnes rectos angulos inter se aequales esse.

And that all right angles are equal to one another.

πάσας / omnes means ‘all’. Greek εἶναι and Latin esse are infinitives, continuing the opening request rather than issuing a new drawing command.

Printed source: Greek pp. 8; Latin pp. 9.

Common notion 3 · γʹ / III · Subtract equals from equals

Καὶ ἐὰν ἀπὸ ἴσων ἴσα ἀφαιρεθῇ, τὰ καταλειπόμενα ἐστιν ἴσα.

Et, si ab aequalibus aequalia subtrahuntur, reliqua sunt aequalia.

And if equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.

ἀπό takes the genitive ἴσων; Latin ab takes ablative aequalibus. τὰ καταλειπόμενα is a participle used as a noun: ‘the things left behind’. In the propositions, λοιπή similarly names what remains.

Printed source: Greek pp. 10; Latin pp. 11.

Common notion 5 in the usual list · ηʹ / VIII in Heiberg · Whole and proper part

Καὶ τὸ ὅλον τοῦ μέρους μεῖζόν [ἐστιν].

Et totum parte maius est.

And the whole [is] greater than the part.

Greek uses the genitive τοῦ μέρους after the comparative μεῖζον; Latin uses the ablative parte after maius. Both mean ‘greater than the part’. The brackets around [ἐστιν] belong to the printed Greek.

Printed source: Greek pp. 10; Latin pp. 11.
