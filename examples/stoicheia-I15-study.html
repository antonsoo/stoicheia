STOICHEIA · EUCLID, BOOK I

I.15 · Subtract a shared angle to find the opposite pair

If two straight lines cut one another, they make the vertically opposite angles equal to one another.

∠ΓΕΑ + ∠ΑΕΔ = two right angles (I.13) · ∠ΑΕΔ + ∠ΔΕΒ = two right angles (I.13) · ∠ΓΕΑ + ∠ΑΕΔ = ∠ΑΕΔ + ∠ΔΕΒ · Subtract ∠ΑΕΔ: ∠ΓΕΑ = ∠ΒΕΔ · Likewise: ∠ΓΕΒ = ∠ΔΕΑ

The proposition

Ἐὰν δύο εὐθεῖαι τέμνωσιν ἀλλήλας, τὰς κατὰ κορυφὴν γωνίας ἴσας ἀλλήλαις ποιοῦσιν.

Si duae rectae inter se secant, angulos ad uerticem positos inter se aequales efficiunt.

If two straight lines cut one another, they make the vertically opposite angles equal to one another.

1. Let two straight lines cross at Ε Given

Δύο γὰρ εὐθεῖαι αἱ ΑΒ, ΓΔ τεμνέτωσαν ἀλλήλας κατὰ τὸ Ε σημεῖον· λέγω, ὅτι ἴση ἐστὶν ἡ μὲν ὑπὸ ΑΕΓ γωνία τῇ ὑπὸ ΔΕΒ, ἡ δὲ ὑπὸ ΓΕΒ τῇ ὑπὸ ΑΕΔ.

Nam duae rectae ΑΒ, ΓΔ inter se secent in puncto Ε. dico, esse ∠ ΑΕΓ = ΔΕΒ et ∠ ΓΕΒ = ΑΕΔ.

Let ΑΒ and ΓΔ cut one another at Ε. I claim that ΑΕΓ equals ΔΕΒ and ΓΕΒ equals ΑΕΔ.

ἡ μέν … ἡ δέ names the first angle and then the second. In every three-letter angle name, Ε in the middle identifies the common vertex. Opposite pairs do not share a ray.

Reason: The task and its given objects A given object is part of the problem. It has not been constructed by this proof. A finite straight line here means a line segment.

Printed source: Greek pp. 40; Latin pp. 41.

2. Use the linear pair on ΓΔ Prove

Ἐπεὶ γὰρ εὐθεῖα ἡ ΑΕ ἐπ᾽ εὐθεῖαν τὴν ΓΔ ἐφέστηκε γωνίας ποιοῦσα τὰς ὑπὸ ΓΕΑ, ΑΕΔ, αἱ ἄρα ὑπὸ ΓΕΑ, ΑΕΔ γωνίαι δυσὶν ὀρθαῖς ἴσαι εἰσίν.

nam quoniam recta ΑΕ super rectam ΓΔ erecta est angulos efficiens ΓΕΑ, ΑΕΔ, anguli ΓΕΑ, ΑΕΔ duobus rectis aequales sunt.

ΑΕ stands on ΓΔ, making ΓΕΑ and ΑΕΔ. Thus these two angles together equal two right angles.

ποιοῦσα / efficiens describes ΑΕ making the angles. δυσὶν ὀρθαῖς / duobus rectis compares their sum with two right angles, just as in I.13.

Reason: I.13 · the first pair totals two right angles ΑΕ stands on ΓΔ. Its two adjacent angles ΓΕΑ and ΑΕΔ therefore equal two right angles together. The intersecting lines extend through Ε; no line is merely touching another at an endpoint.

Printed source: Greek pp. 40; Latin pp. 41.

3. Use the linear pair on ΑΒ Prove

πάλιν, ἐπεὶ εὐθεῖα ἡ ΔΕ ἐπ᾽ εὐθεῖαν τὴν ΑΒ ἐφέστηκε γωνίας ποιοῦσα τὰς ὑπὸ ΑΕΔ, ΔΕΒ, αἱ ἄρα ὑπὸ ΑΕΔ, ΔΕΒ γωνίαι δυσὶν ὀρθαῖς ἴσαι εἰσίν.

rursus quoniam recta ΔΕ super rectam ΑΒ erecta est angulos efficiens ΑΕΔ, ΔΕΒ, anguli ΑΕΔ, ΔΕΒ duobus rectis aequales sunt.

Again, ΔΕ stands on ΑΒ, making ΑΕΔ and ΔΕΒ. This second pair also equals two right angles together.

πάλιν / rursus repeats I.13 with a different line standing on a different base. ΑΕΔ occurs in both pairs; it will be removed from both.

Reason: I.13 · the second pair has the same total ΔΕ stands on ΑΒ. The pair ΑΕΔ and ΔΕΒ also totals two right angles. ΑΕΔ is common to both pairs, even when the two lines meet obliquely.

Printed source: Greek pp. 40; Latin pp. 41.

4. The two pairs have the same total Prove

ἐδείχθησαν δὲ καὶ αἱ ὑπὸ ΓΕΑ, ΑΕΔ δυσὶν ὀρθαῖς ἴσαι· αἱ ἄρα ὑπὸ ΓΕΑ, ΑΕΔ ταῖς ὑπὸ ΑΕΔ, ΔΕΒ ἴσαι εἰσίν.

sed demonstratum est, etiam angulos ΓΕΑ, ΑΕΔ duobus rectis aequales esse. quare ΓΕΑ + ΑΕΔ = ΑΕΔ + ΔΕΒ.

ΓΕΑ and ΑΕΔ were also shown equal to two right angles together. Hence ΓΕΑ plus ΑΕΔ equals ΑΕΔ plus ΔΕΒ.

ἐδείχθησαν / demonstratum est refers back to the first pair. Greek names the paired angles in prose; the Latin now makes the equality of sums explicit with plus signs.

Reason: Common notion 1 · equal totals Both pairs equal two right angles, so the pairs equal one another. This compares totals, not the individual adjacent angles.

Printed source: Greek pp. 40; Latin pp. 41.

5. Subtract ΑΕΔ and compare the remainders Prove

κοινὴ ἀφῃρήσθω ἡ ὑπὸ ΑΕΔ· λοιπὴ ἄρα ἡ ὑπὸ ΓΕΑ λοιπῇ τῇ ὑπὸ ΒΕΔ ἴση ἐστίν·

subtrahatur, qui communis est, ∠ ΑΕΔ. itaque ΓΕΑ = ΒΕΔ.

Subtract the common angle ΑΕΔ. The remaining angle ΓΕΑ therefore equals the remaining angle ΒΕΔ.

λοιπή … λοιπῇ marks the two remainders after subtraction. ΒΕΔ reverses the outer letters of ΔΕΒ and names the same angle.

Reason: Common notion 3 · subtract the shared angle Remove ΑΕΔ from both pairs. The two remaining angles, ΓΕΑ and ΒΕΔ, are vertically opposite and equal. Repeat with the other shared angle to obtain the second opposite pair.

Printed source: Greek pp. 40; Latin pp. 41.

6. Apply the same reasoning to the other pair Prove

ὁμοίως δὴ δειχθήσεται, ὅτι καὶ αἱ ὑπὸ ΓΕΒ, ΔΕΑ ἴσαι εἰσίν.

similiter demonstrabimus, esse etiam ∠ ΓΕΒ = ΔΕΑ.

In the same way, ΓΕΒ and ΔΕΑ will also be shown equal.

ὁμοίως / similiter means ‘in the same way’. The source abbreviates the repeated argument. The plate changes the emphasized pair so that ‘also’ refers to a visibly different pair.

Reason: Common notion 3 · subtract the shared angle Remove ΑΕΔ from both pairs. The two remaining angles, ΓΕΑ and ΒΕΔ, are vertically opposite and equal. Repeat with the other shared angle to obtain the second opposite pair.

Printed source: Greek pp. 40; Latin pp. 41.

7. Both vertically opposite pairs are equal Conclude

Ἐὰν ἄρα δύο εὐθεῖαι τέμνωσιν ἀλλήλας, τὰς κατὰ κορυφὴν γωνίας ἴσας ἀλλήλαις ποιοῦσιν· ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι.

Ergo si duae rectae inter se secant, angulos ad uerticem positos inter se aequales efficiunt; quod erat demonstrandum.

Therefore two straight lines cutting one another make the vertically opposite angles equal. This is what had to be shown.

κατὰ κορυφήν / ad uerticem describes opposition at the vertex. Neither expression says that the lines must be horizontal or vertical; flip the plate or change the angle to check the placement.

Reason: The construction is complete The requested object has been constructed and its defining equality has been proved. The closing phrase says that the task was to make something, rather than merely state a theorem.

Printed source: Greek pp. 40; Latin pp. 41.

Equality by subtraction

The equality does not depend on how the crossing looks. Two linear pairs share a total and an angle, so their remainders are equal. A corollary printed only in the Greek

[Πόρισμα. Ἐκ δὴ τούτου φανερὸν ὅτι, ἐὰν δύο εὐθεῖαι τέμνωσιν ἀλλήλας, τὰς πρὸς τῇ τομῇ γωνίας τέτρασιν ὀρθαῖς ἴσας ποιήσουσιν.]

[Corollary. It is clear from this that, if two straight lines cut one another, they make the angles at the intersection equal to four right angles together.]

Heiberg brackets this whole corollary on Greek p. 42. His facing Latin begins I.16 and provides no corresponding translation. The English here is our reading translation; no missing Latin has been supplied. The critical apparatus records differing witnesses. Greek source pp. 42.

Definitions and starting permissions used in this proof

Common notion 1 · αʹ / I · Equality through a shared equal

Τὰ τῷ αὐτῷ ἴσα καὶ ἀλλήλοις ἐστὶν ἴσα.

Quae eidem aequalia sunt, etiam inter se aequalia sunt.

Things equal to the same thing are also equal to one another.

τῷ αὐτῷ and eidem mean ‘to the same [thing]’. The Greek neuter plural τὰ … ἴσα takes the singular ἐστίν, a normal Greek construction. Latin uses plural sunt.

Printed source: Greek pp. 10; Latin pp. 11.

Postulate 4 · δʹ / IV · Right angles are equal

Καὶ πάσας τὰς ὀρθὰς γωνίας ἴσας ἀλλήλαις εἶναι.

Et omnes rectos angulos inter se aequales esse.

And that all right angles are equal to one another.

πάσας / omnes means ‘all’. Greek εἶναι and Latin esse are infinitives, continuing the opening request rather than issuing a new drawing command.

Printed source: Greek pp. 8; Latin pp. 9.

Common notion 3 · γʹ / III · Subtract equals from equals

Καὶ ἐὰν ἀπὸ ἴσων ἴσα ἀφαιρεθῇ, τὰ καταλειπόμενα ἐστιν ἴσα.

Et, si ab aequalibus aequalia subtrahuntur, reliqua sunt aequalia.

And if equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.

ἀπό takes the genitive ἴσων; Latin ab takes ablative aequalibus. τὰ καταλειπόμενα is a participle used as a noun: ‘the things left behind’. In the propositions, λοιπή similarly names what remains.

Printed source: Greek pp. 10; Latin pp. 11.
