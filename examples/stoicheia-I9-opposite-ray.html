I.9 · Construct an angle’s equal halves

Divide a given rectilinear angle into two equal parts.

Choose the crossing across ΔΕ from Α: the selected ray is outside the given angle.

Other crossing · equal angles outside the given angle

Above 60°, this crossing lies on the opposite bisector ray. Its two angles with ΑΔ and ΑΕ are equal, but their ray lies outside ∠ΒΑΓ and does not divide it. Choose the crossing across ΔΕ from Α to construct the internal bisector.

Equal sides · the ray is outside the angle
ΑΔΖΑΕΖCorrespondence
ΑΔΑΕcut equal using I.3
ΔΖΕΖequilateral construction
ΑΖΑΖcommon side

ΑΔ = ΑΕ (I.3) · ΔΖ = ΕΖ (I.1) · ΑΖ = ΑΖ (common side)

The proposition

Τὴν δοθεῖσαν γωνίαν εὐθύγραμμον δίχα τεμεῖν.

Datum angulum rectilineum in duas partes aequales diuidere.

Divide a given rectilinear angle into two equal parts.

1. Start with the angle to be divided Given

Ἔστω ἡ δοθεῖσα γωνία εὐθύγραμμος ἡ ὑπὸ ΒΑΓ. δεῖ δὴ αὐτὴν δίχα τεμεῖν.

Sit datus angulus rectilineus ΒΑΓ: oportet igitur eum in duas partes aequales diuidere.

Let ΒΑΓ be the given rectilinear angle. It is to be divided into two equal parts.

δίχα means ‘in two’; the construction requires equal halves, made explicit by Latin in duas partes aequales. τεμεῖν and diuidere are infinitives following the impersonal δεῖ and oportet: ‘it is necessary to divide’.

Reason: The task and its given objects A given object is part of the problem. It has not been constructed by this proof. A finite straight line here means a line segment.

Printed source: Greek pp. 28; Latin pp. 29.

2. Obtain equal lengths on the two rays Construct

Εἰλήφθω ἐπὶ τῆς ΑΒ τυχὸν σημεῖον τὸ Δ, καὶ ἀφῃρήσθω ἀπὸ τῆς ΑΓ τῇ ΑΔ ἴση ἡ ΑΕ,

sumatur in ΑΒ quoduis punctum Δ, et ab ΑΓ rectae ΑΔ aequalis abscindatur ΑΕ,

Choose a point Δ on ΑΒ, and cut ΑΕ from ΑΓ equal to ΑΔ.

τυχὸν / quoduis allows a chosen point. The practical plate keeps it closer to Α than either displayed ray endpoint, so I.3 can cut the required part. ἀπὸ τῆς ΑΓ identifies the segment being cut; τῇ ΑΔ identifies the length to match.

Reason: I.3 · obtain equal lengths on the two rays Choose Δ on ΑΒ closer to Α than the available endpoint Γ. I.3 then cuts ΑΕ equal to ΑΔ from ΑΓ. The model actually calls that earlier construction; it does not choose Ε by visually reflecting Δ.

Printed source: Greek pp. 28; Latin pp. 29.

3. Join the two obtained points Construct

καὶ ἐπεζεύχθω ἡ ΔΕ,

et ducatur ΔΕ,

Join Δ and Ε.

The repeated καί / et joins consecutive operations. ΔΕ becomes the base on which the next operation invokes I.1.

Reason: Postulate 1 · join two points A straight line may be drawn from any point to any point. The straightedge is unmarked: this operation does not measure a length.

Printed source: Greek pp. 28; Latin pp. 29.

4. Construct the equilateral triangle Construct

καὶ συνεστάτω ἐπὶ τῆς ΔΕ τρίγωνον ἰσόπλευρον τὸ ΔΕΖ,

et in ΔΕ construatur triangulus aequilaterus ΔΕΖ,

Construct the equilateral triangle ΔΕΖ on ΔΕ.

ἐπὶ τῆς ΔΕ / in ΔΕ means ‘on ΔΕ’ as a base. The words name the equilateral construction; the printed figure supplies the intended side for Ζ. The plate states that choice explicitly rather than silently treating the two crossings as interchangeable.

Reason: I.1 · construct an equilateral triangle on ΔΕ The two circles of I.1 supply two possible vertices. Choose Ζ on the side of ΔΕ opposite Α, as in the printed figure. This keeps ΑΖ inside the given angle. The alternative crossing can work, collapse at Α, or put the ray outside the angle; inspect those cases with the plate controls.

Printed source: Greek pp. 28; Latin pp. 29.

5. Join the vertex to Ζ and state the claim Construct

καὶ ἐπεζεύχθω ἡ ΑΖ· λέγω, ὅτι ἡ ὑπὸ ΒΑΓ γωνία δίχα τέτμηται ὑπὸ τῆς ΑΖ εὐθείας.

et ducatur ΑΖ. dico, angulum ΒΑΓ recta ΑΖ in duas partes aequales diuisum esse.

Join Α to Ζ. I claim that ΑΖ has divided angle ΒΑΓ into equal halves.

τέτμηται and diuisum esse present the proposed completed result. Because they follow λέγω / dico, they announce what the following proof must establish; drawing the line has not yet proved the equality.

Reason: Postulate 1 · join two points A straight line may be drawn from any point to any point. The straightedge is unmarked: this operation does not measure a length.

Printed source: Greek pp. 28; Latin pp. 29.

6. Identify two corresponding side pairs Prove

Ἐπεὶ γὰρ ἴση ἐστὶν ἡ ΑΔ τῇ ΑΕ, κοινὴ δὲ ἡ ΑΖ, δύο δὴ αἱ ΔΑ, ΑΖ δυσὶ ταῖς ΕΑ, ΑΖ ἴσαι εἰσὶν ἑκατέρα ἑκατέρᾳ.

nam cum ΑΔ = ΑΕ, et ΑΖ communis sit, duae rectae ΔΑ, ΑΖ duabus ΕΑ, ΑΖ aequales sunt altera alteri;

ΑΔ equals ΑΕ and ΑΖ is common. Thus ΔΑ and ΑΖ equal ΕΑ and ΑΖ respectively.

ΔΑ names the same segment as ΑΔ. The reversal follows the order of the compared triangles, ΔΑΖ and ΕΑΖ. κοινή / communis identifies a single side used in both triangles, not two separately constructed equal lines.

Reason: The obtained equal lengths and one common side I.3 supplied ΑΔ = ΑΕ. ΑΖ belongs to both triangles: comparing it with itself does not require another construction. These are two of the three side pairs needed for I.8.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

7. Supply the third side pair Prove

καὶ βάσις ἡ ΔΖ βάσει τῇ ΕΖ ἴση ἐστίν·

et basis ΔΖ basi ΕΖ aequalis est.

The base ΔΖ also equals the base ΕΖ.

βάσις / basis now names the side opposite the angle being compared in each smaller triangle. ‘Base’ is a role in the argument, not necessarily the horizontal line in the picture.

Reason: The remaining equal sides come from I.1 ΔΖ and ΕΖ are sides of the equilateral triangle ΔΕΖ. Together with ΑΔ = ΑΕ and common ΑΖ, this supplies every side pair of triangles ΔΑΖ and ΕΑΖ.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

8. Use I.8 to obtain the equal angle parts Prove

γωνία ἄρα ἡ ὑπὸ ΔΑΖ γωνίᾳ τῇ ὑπὸ ΕΑΖ ἴση ἐστίν.

itaque ∠ ΔΑΖ = ΕΑΖ.

Therefore angle ΔΑΖ equals angle ΕΑΖ.

Both angle names have Α in the middle. The outer letters change from Δ to Ε while Ζ remains common, matching the two sides of the dividing ray. ἄρα / itaque signals the consequence of the three established side pairs.

Reason: I.8 · three side pairs give the equal angles Compare ΔΑΖ with ΕΑΖ: ΔΑ = ΕΑ, ΑΖ is common, and ΔΖ = ΕΖ. I.8 gives ∠ΔΑΖ = ∠ΕΑΖ. The chosen position of Ζ places these adjacent angles inside the given angle, so their equality really bisects it.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

9. The angle has been bisected Conclude

Ἡ ἄρα δοθεῖσα γωνία εὐθύγραμμος ἡ ὑπὸ ΒΑΓ δίχα τέτμηται ὑπὸ τῆς ΑΖ εὐθείας· ὅπερ ἔδει ποιῆσαι.

Ergo datus angulus rectilineus ΒΑΓ recta ΑΖ in duas partes aequales diuisus est; quod oportebat fieri.

The given rectilinear angle ΒΑΓ has therefore been divided into equal halves by ΑΖ. The requested construction is complete.

The given object returns with δοθεῖσα / datus, and the completed division with τέτμηται / diuisus est. ποιῆσαι / fieri closes a construction problem; compare δεῖξαι / demonstrandum in the preceding theorems.

Reason: The construction is complete The requested object has been constructed and its defining equality has been proved. The closing phrase says that the task was to make something, rather than merely state a theorem.

Printed source: Greek pp. 30; Latin pp. 31.

Equal angles still need the right position

I.1 gives two circle intersections. The one across ΔΕ from Α always gives an internal dividing ray. For the other crossing, an angle below 60° still gives an internal ray; at 60° the crossing is Α itself; above 60° it gives the opposite ray. In that last case the two measured angles are equal, but they are not the two parts of the original angle. The printed figure encodes a position choice that the short command does not spell out.

Definitions and starting permissions used in this proof

Postulate 1 · αʹ / I · Join two points

Ἠιτήσθω ἀπὸ παντὸς σημείου ἐπὶ πᾶν σημεῖον εὐθεῖαν γραμμὴν ἀγαγεῖν.

Postuletur, ut a quouis puncto ad quoduis punctum recta linea ducatur.

Let it be postulated that a straight line can be drawn from any point to any point.

Ἠιτήσθω / Postuletur means ‘let it be requested / postulated’. The Greek then uses an infinitive, ἀγαγεῖν (‘to draw’); Latin uses ut … ducatur, ‘that … be drawn’. The following postulates continue the opening request.

Printed source: Greek pp. 8; Latin pp. 9.

Definition 20 · κʹ / XX · Triangles classified by their sides

Τῶν δὲ τριπλεύρων σχημάτων ἰσόπλευρον μὲν τρίγωνόν ἐστι τὸ τὰς τρεῖς ἴσας ἔχον πλευράς, ἰσοσκελὲς δὲ τὸ τὰς δύο μόνας ἴσας ἔχον πλευράς, σκαληνὸν δὲ τὸ τὰς τρεῖς ἀνίσους ἔχον πλευράς.

Ex figuris autem trilateris aequilaterus triangulus est, qui tria latera sua aequalia habet, aequicrurius uero, qui duo sola aequalia habet, scalenus autem, qui tria latera sua inaequalia habet.

Of three-sided figures, an equilateral triangle has its three sides equal; an isosceles triangle has only two sides equal; a scalene triangle has its three sides unequal.

μόνας / sola means ‘only’. This edition’s definition makes the isosceles and equilateral classes separate. Many modern courses include equilateral triangles among isosceles triangles. The conclusion of I.5 remains true when all three sides are equal. The scan’s right edge on p. 6 is clipped; the Greek was also checked against the quoted text in Heath’s commentary (see the editorial note).

Printed source: Greek pp. 6; Latin pp. 7.
