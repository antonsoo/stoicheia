I.8 · Let three sides determine the angle

When two triangles have two respective sides equal and their bases equal too, the angles enclosed by those equal sides will also be equal.

Align the equal bases

ΒΓ aligns with ΕΖ. Their equal endpoint distances then force Α onto Δ on the chosen side of the base. The included angle is calculated from the three lengths; it is not an extra given. As in I.4, the motion illustrates superposition, an operation not explicitly licensed by the five postulates.

Three side pairs · the angle is earned
ΑΒΓΔΕΖCorrespondence
ΑΒΔΕfirst equal pair
ΑΓΔΖsecond equal pair
ΒΓΕΖequal bases
ΑΔmeeting point fixed by I.7 after bases align

ΑΒ = ΔΕ; ΑΓ = ΔΖ; ΒΓ = ΕΖ (given) · ∠ΒΑΓ = ∠ΕΔΖ (conclusion)

The proposition

Ἐὰν δύο τρίγωνα τὰς δύο πλευρὰς [ταῖς] δύο πλευραῖς ἴσας ἔχῃ ἑκατέραν ἑκατέρᾳ, ἔχῃ δὲ καὶ τὴν βάσιν τῇ βάσει ἴσην, καὶ τὴν γωνίαν τῇ γωνίᾳ ἴσην ἕξει τὴν ὑπὸ τῶν ἴσων εὐθειῶν περιεχομένην.

Si duo trianguli duo latera duobus lateribus aequalia habent alterum alteri et praeterea basim basi aequalem habent, etiam angulos aequalibus rectis comprehensos aequales habebunt.

When two triangles have two respective sides equal and their bases equal too, the angles enclosed by those equal sides will also be equal.

1. Name the three side pairs and the angle to prove Given

Ἔστω δύο τρίγωνα τὰ ΑΒΓ, ΔΕΖ τὰς δύο πλευρὰς τὰς ΑΒ, ΑΓ ταῖς δύο πλευραῖς ταῖς ΔΕ, ΔΖ ἴσας ἔχοντα ἑκατέραν ἑκατέρᾳ, τὴν μὲν ΑΒ τῇ ΔΕ τὴν δὲ ΑΓ τῇ ΔΖ· ἐχέτω δὲ καὶ βάσιν τὴν ΒΓ βάσει τῇ ΕΖ ἴσην· λέγω, ὅτι καὶ γωνία ἡ ὑπὸ ΒΑΓ γωνίᾳ τῇ ὑπὸ ΕΔΖ ἐστιν ἴση.

Sint duo trianguli ΑΒΓ, ΔΕΖ duo latera ΑΒ, ΑΓ duobus lateribus ΔΕ, ΔΖ aequalia habentes alterum alteri, ΑΒ = ΔΕ et ΑΓ = ΔΖ, et praeterea habeant ΒΓ = ΕΖ. dico, etiam esse ∠ ΒΑΓ = ΕΔΖ.

Let ΑΒΓ and ΔΕΖ have ΑΒ = ΔΕ, ΑΓ = ΔΖ and ΒΓ = ΕΖ. I claim that angle ΒΑΓ also equals angle ΕΔΖ.

ἐχέτω / habeant adds the equal-base condition. Compare I.4: there the included angle was given and the equal base was proved. Here those roles are exchanged. λέγω / dico introduces a claim still to be justified.

Reason: The task and its given objects A given object is part of the problem. It has not been constructed by this proof. A finite straight line here means a line segment.

Printed source: Greek pp. 26; Latin pp. 27.

2. Apply one equal base to the other Prove

Ἐφαρμοζομένου γὰρ τοῦ ΑΒΓ τριγώνου ἐπὶ τὸ ΔΕΖ τρίγωνον καὶ τιθεμένου τοῦ μὲν Β σημείου ἐπὶ τὸ Ε σημεῖον τῆς δὲ ΒΓ εὐθείας ἐπὶ τὴν ΕΖ ἐφαρμόσει καὶ τὸ Γ σημεῖον ἐπὶ τὸ Ζ διὰ τὸ ἴσην εἶναι τὴν ΒΓ τῇ ΕΖ·

nam triangulo ΑΒΓ ad triangulum ΔΕΖ adplicato et puncto Β in Ε puncto posito recta autem ΒΓ in ΕΖ etiam Γ punctum in Ζ cadet, quia ΒΓ = ΕΖ.

Apply ΑΒΓ to ΔΕΖ, placing Β on Ε and ΒΓ along ΕΖ. Then Γ falls on Ζ because the bases are equal.

The Greek genitive absolutes ἐφαρμοζομένου and τιθεμένου set up the placement. Heiberg’s Latin uses ablative absolutes adplicato and posito. The corresponding endpoints are now Β → Ε and Γ → Ζ.

Reason: Superposition · an additional assumption Euclid applies one triangle to the other while keeping its sides and angles unchanged. His five postulates do not explicitly license that operation. The movable plate illustrates a rigid motion in the real plane; it does not fill this axiomatic gap. Reflected correspondences need a reflection too.

Printed source: Greek pp. 26; Latin pp. 27.

3. Claim that the remaining sides must coincide Prove

ἐφαρμοσάσης δὴ τῆς ΒΓ ἐπὶ τὴν ΕΖ ἐφαρμόσουσι καὶ αἱ ΒΑ, ΓΑ ἐπὶ τὰς ΕΔ, ΔΖ.

adplicata iam ΒΓ rectae ΕΖ etiam ΒΑ, ΓΑ cum ΕΔ, ΔΖ congruent.

Once ΒΓ is on ΕΖ, the sides ΒΑ and ΓΑ must also coincide with ΕΔ and ΔΖ. The next step explains why.

The future ἐφαρμόσουσι / congruent states what must follow from applying the base. ΓΑ is the same segment as ΑΓ, named from the base endpoint here. The English last sentence marks the source’s claim-then-justification structure.

Reason: I.7 · one meeting point on the same side After the equal bases have been aligned, two distinct meeting points on the same side cannot have the same two distances from the corresponding base endpoints. The side condition matters: the two circle intersections on opposite sides are both possible.

Printed source: Greek pp. 26, 28; Latin pp. 27, 29.

4. Suppose the sides meet at another apex Prove

εἰ γὰρ βάσις μὲν ἡ ΒΓ ἐπὶ βάσιν τὴν ΕΖ ἐφαρμόσει, αἱ δὲ ΒΑ, ΑΓ πλευραὶ ἐπὶ τὰς ΕΔ, ΔΖ οὐκ ἐφαρμόσουσιν ἀλλὰ παραλλάξουσιν ὡς αἱ ΕΗ, ΗΖ, συσταθήσονται ἐπὶ τῆς αὐτῆς εὐθείας δύο ταῖς αὐταῖς εὐθείαις ἄλλαι δύο εὐθεῖαι ἴσαι ἑκατέρα ἑκατέρᾳ πρὸς ἄλλῳ καὶ ἄλλῳ σημείῳ ἐπὶ τὰ αὐτὰ μέρη τὰ αὐτὰ πέρατα ἔχουσαι.

nam si basis ΒΓ cum basi ΕΖ congruet, latera autem ΒΑ, ΑΓ cum ΕΔ, ΔΖ non congruent, uerum extra cadent, ut ΕΗ, ΗΖ, in eadem recta iisdem duabus rectis aliae duae rectae aequales altera alteri constituentur ad aliud atque aliud punctum ad eandem partem eosdem terminos habentes.

Suppose the bases coincide but the other sides fall elsewhere, along ΕΗ and ΗΖ. There would then be a different meeting point Η on the same side of ΕΖ, with the same two lengths from the same respective endpoints.

μὲν … δὲ separates what coincides (the base) from what is supposed not to coincide (the other sides). παραλλάξουσιν / extra cadent introduces a displaced candidate. The long final clause deliberately repeats the conditions of I.7.

Reason: I.7 · one meeting point on the same side After the equal bases have been aligned, two distinct meeting points on the same side cannot have the same two distances from the corresponding base endpoints. The side condition matters: the two circle intersections on opposite sides are both possible.

Printed source: Greek pp. 28; Latin pp. 29.

But I.7 rules out such a second pair on the same side.

This brief denial relies on the entire description just given. The English names I.7 to make the reference explicit; Heiberg prints the proposition reference beside the Latin.

6. Unpack the double negative Prove

οὐκ ἄρα ἐφαρμοζομένης τῆς ΒΓ βάσεως ἐπὶ τὴν ΕΖ βάσιν οὐκ ἐφαρμόσουσι καὶ αἱ ΒΑ, ΑΓ πλευραὶ ἐπὶ τὰς ΕΔ, ΔΖ. ἐφαρμόσουσιν ἄρα·

itaque fieri non potest, ut basi ΒΓ ad basim ΕΖ adplicata non congruant etiam latera ΒΑ, ΑΓ cum ΕΔ, ΔΖ. congruent igitur.

Therefore, with the bases aligned, it cannot be that the remaining sides fail to coincide with ΕΔ and ΔΖ. They must coincide.

The outer οὐκ denies the proposed failure expressed by the inner οὐκ. Latin separates the scopes clearly: non potest … non congruant, ‘it cannot happen that they do not coincide’. The final short clause gives the positive result.

Reason: I.7 · one meeting point on the same side After the equal bases have been aligned, two distinct meeting points on the same side cannot have the same two distances from the corresponding base endpoints. The side condition matters: the two circle intersections on opposite sides are both possible.

Printed source: Greek pp. 28; Latin pp. 29.

7. Conclude equality of the included angles Prove

ὥστε καὶ γωνία ἡ ὑπὸ ΒΑΓ ἐπὶ γωνίαν τὴν ὑπὸ ΕΔΖ ἐφαρμόσει καὶ ἴση αὐτῇ ἔσται.

quare etiam angulus ΒΑΓ cum angulo ΕΔΖ congruet et ei aequalis erit.

Consequently angle ΒΑΓ coincides with angle ΕΔΖ and is equal to it.

αὐτῇ and ei are datives referring to the angle ΕΔΖ: ‘equal to it’. The angle equality is the result, not an extra premise supplied by the drawing.

Reason: I.7 excludes the second apex; coincidence gives equality With Β on Ε and Γ on Ζ, the remaining vertex must fall on Δ on the selected side. The angle then coincides with ∠ΕΔΖ and is equal to it. The source still relies on superposition, as I.4 did; the three numerical side checks do not justify that assumption.

Printed source: Greek pp. 28; Latin pp. 29.

8. Three side pairs have determined the angle Conclude

Ἐὰν ἄρα δύο τρίγωνα τὰς δύο πλευρὰς [ταῖς] δύο πλευραῖς ἴσας ἔχῃ ἑκατέραν ἑκατέρᾳ καὶ τὴν βάσιν τῇ βάσει ἴσην ἔχῃ, καὶ τὴν γωνίαν τῇ γωνίᾳ ἴσην ἕξει τὴν ὑπὸ τῶν ἴσων εὐθειῶν περιεχομένην· ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι.

Ergo si duo trianguli duo latera duobus lateribus aequalia habent alterum alteri et basim basi aequalem habent, etiam angulos aequalibus rectis comprehensos aequales habebunt; quod erat demonstrandum.

Thus two triangles with those three respective side pairs equal have equal angles between the first two sides. Which was to be shown.

The bracketed [ταῖς] remains as in Heiberg’s printed Greek. The result’s future ἕξει / habebunt returns to the general statement, beyond the particular labelled triangles.

Reason: The theorem is proved The conclusion follows from the stated givens. ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι and quod erat demonstrandum close a proof: ‘which was to be shown’. Compare ποιῆσαι, ‘to make’, at the end of the first three construction problems.

Printed source: Greek pp. 28; Latin pp. 29.

The angle is earned, not set as a hidden given

The model reconstructs an apex from three lengths. You can enter lengths that do not close into a triangle; the reader then withholds the triangle. When they do close, I.7 supplies the source argument for the apex after the equal bases are applied. The motion still illustrates an additional superposition assumption. Changing the data is a numerical exploration, not a replacement proof of I.7.

Definitions and starting permissions used in this proof

Common notion 4 in the usual list · ζʹ / VII in Heiberg · Coincidence gives equality

Καὶ τὰ ἐφαρμόζοντα ἐπ’ ἄλληλα ἴσα ἀλλήλοις ἐστίν.

Et quae inter se congruunt, aequalia sunt.

And things that coincide with one another are equal to one another.

The printed number is seven because Heiberg’s Greek includes three bracketed extra common notions before it. His facing Latin omits those entries but retains VII. This reader calls it common notion 4 and makes the edition number visible.

Printed source: Greek pp. 10; Latin pp. 11.
