I.19 · Recover the greater side from the greater angle

In any triangle, the side opposite the greater angle is greater.

IF ΑΓ = ΑΒ, THEN ∠ΑΒΓ = ∠ΑΓΒ (I.5) · This contradicts the original greater-angle premise

The proposition

Παντὸς τριγώνου ὑπὸ τὴν μείζονα γωνίαν ἡ μείζων πλευρὰ ὑποτείνει.

In quouis triangulo sub maiore angulo maius latus subtendit.

In any triangle, the side opposite the greater angle is greater.

1. Begin with the angle inequality Given

Ἔστω τρίγωνον τὸ ΑΒΓ μείζονα ἔχον τὴν ὑπὸ ΑΒΓ γωνίαν τῆς ὑπὸ ΒΓΑ· λέγω, ὅτι καὶ πλευρὰ ἡ ΑΓ πλευρᾶς τῆς ΑΒ μείζων ἐστίν.

Sit triangulus ΑΒΓ habens ∠ ΑΒΓ > ΒΓΑ. dico, etiam esse ΑΓ > ΑΒ.

Let ΑΒΓ be a triangle with angle ΑΒΓ greater than ΒΓΑ. I say that side ΑΓ is also greater than ΑΒ.

The given and the goal have exchanged roles since I.18. μείζονα ἔχον now introduces an angle inequality. The shape is a numerical realization of that given, not a proof of the converse.

Reason: The task and its given objects A given object is part of the problem. It has not been constructed by this proof. A finite straight line here means a line segment.

Printed source: Greek pp. 46; Latin pp. 47.

2. State the two alternatives Prove

Εἰ γὰρ μή, ἤτοι ἴση ἐστὶν ἡ ΑΓ τῇ ΑΒ ἢ ἐλάσσων·

nam si minus, aut ΑΓ = ΑΒ aut ΑΓ < ΑΒ.

For if not, ΑΓ is either equal to ΑΒ or less than it.

Εἰ γὰρ μή / nam si minus means ‘for if not’ here. Latin minus does not itself assert the shorter-side case: the following aut … aut gives both alternatives.

Reason: Exclude the other two possible orders For two lengths exactly one of greater, equal or less holds. If ΑΓ were not greater than ΑΒ, it would be equal or less. Each alternative must be tested against the GIVEN angle inequality; a converse is not proved merely by reversing a sentence.

Printed source: Greek pp. 46; Latin pp. 47.

3. Reject equal sides Prove

ἴση μὲν οὖν οὐκ ἔστιν ἡ ΑΓ τῇ ΑΒ· ἴση γὰρ ἂν ἦν καὶ γωνία ἡ ὑπὸ ΑΒΓ τῇ ὑπὸ ΑΓΒ· οὐκ ἔστι δέ· οὐκ ἄρα ἴση ἐστὶν ἡ ΑΓ τῇ ΑΒ.

iam non est ΑΓ = ΑΒ. tum enim esset ∠ ΑΒΓ = ΑΓΒ; uerum non est. itaque non est ΑΓ = ΑΒ.

ΑΓ is not equal to ΑΒ: then angle ΑΒΓ would also equal ΑΓΒ. But it does not. Therefore ΑΓ is not equal to ΑΒ.

ἂν ἦν and esset express what would follow under the supposition. The equal angles belong to the hypothetical case; they are not new facts about the original given triangle.

Reason: I.5 · the equal-side alternative contradicts the given angles If ΑΓ = ΑΒ, I.5 forces ΑΒΓ = ΑΓΒ. That conflicts with the given ΑΒΓ > ΑΓΒ. The plate temporarily draws the equal-side case and explicitly withdraws the original angle premise from that altered drawing.

Printed source: Greek pp. 46; Latin pp. 47.

4. Reject the shorter side Prove

οὐδὲ μὴν ἐλάσσων ἐστὶν ἡ ΑΓ τῆς ΑΒ· ἐλάσσων γὰρ ἂν ἦν καὶ γωνία ἡ ὑπὸ ΑΒΓ τῆς ὑπὸ ΑΓΒ· οὐκ ἔστι δέ· οὐκ ἄρα ἐλάσσων ἐστὶν ἡ ΑΓ τῆς ΑΒ.

neque uero ΑΓ < ΑΒ. tum enim esset ∠ ΑΒΓ < ΑΓΒ. uerum non est. itaque non est ΑΓ < ΑΒ.

Nor is ΑΓ less than ΑΒ: then angle ΑΒΓ would also be less than ΑΓΒ. But it is not. Therefore ΑΓ is not less than ΑΒ.

οὐδὲ μήν / neque uero introduces the second rejection. I.18 is applied with the side roles reversed. The repeated οὐκ ἔστι δέ / uerum non est points back to the given angle order.

Reason: I.18 · the shorter-side alternative reverses the given order If ΑΓ < ΑΒ, apply I.18 to the longer side ΑΒ: its opposite angle ΑΓΒ exceeds ΑΒΓ. This again contradicts the given order. The altered triangle illustrates the consequence of this supposition, not a triangle satisfying every premise.

Printed source: Greek pp. 46, 48; Latin pp. 47, 49.

5. Keep the only remaining order Prove

ἐδείχθη δέ, ὅτι οὐδὲ ἴση ἐστίν. μείζων ἄρα ἐστὶν ἡ ΑΓ τῆς ΑΒ.

demonstratum autem est, ne aequalem quidem esse. quare ΑΓ > ΑΒ.

It was also shown that ΑΓ is not equal to ΑΒ. Therefore ΑΓ is greater than ΑΒ.

ἐδείχθη / demonstratum est recalls an established rejection. ne … quidem reinforces ‘not even equal’. Both failed alternatives are needed before greater follows.

Reason: Only the greater-side case remains Equality and the shorter-side alternative have both contradicted the given angle order. The remaining possibility is ΑΓ > ΑΒ. The plate returns to the original given triangle.

Printed source: Greek pp. 48; Latin pp. 49.

6. Conclude the converse Conclude

Παντὸς ἄρα τριγώνου ὑπὸ τὴν μείζονα γωνίαν ἡ μείζων πλευρὰ ὑποτείνει· ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι.

Ergo in quouis triangulo sub maiore angulo maius latus subtendit; quod erat demonstrandum.

Therefore, in any triangle, the side opposite the greater angle is greater. This is what had to be shown.

ὑπὸ τὴν μείζονα γωνίαν comes before the subject ἡ μείζων πλευρά. The angle is the starting information. Latin likewise moves sub maiore angulo before maius latus.

Reason: Only the greater-side case remains Equality and the shorter-side alternative have both contradicted the given angle order. The remaining possibility is ΑΓ > ΑΒ. The plate returns to the original given triangle.

Printed source: Greek pp. 48; Latin pp. 49.

A converse needs an argument of its own

I.18 goes from sides to angles. I.19 goes back from angles to sides by testing every other side order. The changed sketches in steps 3 and 4 show why those alternatives cannot coexist with the original angle premise. The proof then returns to that original triangle.
